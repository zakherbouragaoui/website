## What You'll Experience

Over two days in April 2026, we traveled more than 1,000 kilometers through Tunisia's biogeographic gradient—from the last acacia stands at Bel Khir to the deep Saharan oasis of Qsar Ghilen and back.

This isn't a story of silence. It's a story of **29 species, 1,210 acoustic detections**, and a desert ecosystem that refuses to be called empty.

## Key Findings

Our passive acoustic monitoring at Qsar Ghilen revealed:

- **29 species detected** (19 confirmed, 10 possible)
- **1,210 total detections** across 17 hours
- **Spanish Sparrow** dominated with 384 detections
- Peak activity at **dawn (05:00-07:00)** and **dusk (17:00-19:00)**
- Mean confidence score: **0.777** (high reliability)

The data refutes the "empty desert" narrative. Where permanent water exists, biodiversity concentrates—creating critical refugia in arid landscapes.

## Technical Details

The storymap uses a **map-first design** inspired by National Geographic's visual storytelling:

- **Map Engine**: Leaflet.js with Stamen Terrain tiles
- **Scroll Framework**: Scrollama + GSAP ScrollTrigger
- **Performance**: Lazy loading, 60fps scroll target
- **Accessibility**: Keyboard navigation, reduced motion support
- **Data**: 774KB GeoJSON route file with 29 waypoints

**Best viewed on desktop** for full glassmorphic effects and wide-screen map experience. Mobile version uses a stacked layout optimized for smaller screens.
